# Balance report — the production corpus

What a run of this generator actually produced, written down so that a figure computed on the corpus
can be read with its denominator beside it. The generator prints this report to stdout; this file is
the copy that gets read by a person.

The machine-readable form of everything below is `run_profiles` profile **RP-05** in
`config/labelling-schema.yaml`. That profile is the authoritative one: it describes the corpus a
consumer receives. Every other profile in that file is an example of how a quantity behaves on some
run, and none of them describes the delivered data.

## The run

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Command | `generate-dataset --seed 42 --personas 48 --claims-per-persona 8` |
| Seed | 42 |
| Generator | receipt-synth 0.1.0 |
| Contract | `config/labelling-schema.yaml` version 19 |
| Jurisdiction | UA (the default; no other is built) |
| Size | 48 personas · 359 of 384 claims built · 666 documents |
| Dataset location | outside this repository — the repo ships the generator, its configuration and the seed |

The 25 claims that were ordered and not built ran out of annual balance: every documentable category
of that persona had its limit spent. That is the limit mechanism working, not a failure.

### Checking a corpus against this report

The identifiers below are over the run's own output. A consumer holding a directory can confirm it is
this corpus rather than another run of the same command:

```text
manifest_sha256   199634e4690178a637f911f79ac4912b7334ab265cfdd39bea01a0f3ef1c3357
images_sha256     5b45c8b52d30cfe939114d9bc5a48ba9a871bcb9ec77b5f021e7c4c8c913b819    666 files
labels_sha256     ad3bb2b318090d948886c317753b14c353a7232efe933268dc6002ccaff8ec78   1025 files
```

`manifest_sha256` is the SHA-256 of `ground_truth.json`. The other two are a SHA-256 over the files of
a directory, each contributing its name followed by the SHA-256 of its bytes, in sorted order by name:

```python
h = hashlib.sha256()
for path in sorted(directory.glob("*")):
    h.update(path.name.encode())
    h.update(hashlib.sha256(path.read_bytes()).digest())
```

## Document classes — the threshold, checked on this run

The minimum is **≥ 30 documents per buildable class**. Below 30, the 95% Wilson interval around an
accuracy near 0.9 is about ±0.10, so "0.91" and "0.85" are indistinguishable and a per-class figure is
not worth quoting. Because macro-F1 weights every class equally, a thin class swings the mean.

**All four target classes clear it on this corpus.** Denominator 666 documents.

| class | documents | share | ≥ 30 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| `invoice` | 307 | 46.1% | yes |
| `bank_statement` | 154 | 23.1% | yes |
| `payment_confirmation` | 153 | 23.0% | yes |
| `fiscal_receipt` | 52 | 7.8% | yes |

The mix is **not balanced and was never tuned to be**. `config/policy.yaml` declares a `verdict_mix`
and deliberately declares no document mix — a share of receipts against invoices would read as an
observation about which documents claimants actually submit, and nothing here has measured that. The
class counts fall out of which verdicts are realizable and which archetypes exist. Macro-F1 needs
sufficient *n* per class, not equal *n*, so the spread above is not a defect.

`other` is outside the threshold and is reported separately: **it is empty.** Three classes named by
the vocabulary — `act`, `order_screenshot`, `non_fiscal_receipt` — have no archetype, so no run of any
size contains one. That is absence, not a count of zero, and the minimum does not apply to them.

## Verdicts

Denominator 359 claims.

| verdict | claims | share | target, of the realizable subset |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `covered` | 165 | 46.0% | 62.5% |
| `partially_covered` | 155 | 43.2% | 25.0% |
| `insufficient_evidence` | 39 | 10.9% | 12.5% |

**20% of the declared mix is absent from this corpus, at least.** `not_proof_of_payment` (10%) and
`partially_paid` (10%) are not generated by any run today, and `rejected` carries no declared share at
all — so the shares above are conditional on the subset that can be drawn, and 20% is a lower bound on
what is missing rather than the whole of it. They are not this dataset's balance against `verdict_mix`
and must not be read as one.

`partially_covered` came out well above target because the oracle overrules the plan: 77 claims drawn
as `covered` were labelled `partially_covered` by a binding annual limit, while 15 drawn as
`partially_covered` came out `covered`. The label is derived, never asserted.

## `insufficient_evidence` — the realized distribution of CAUSES

A verdict's share says nothing about which mechanism produced it. The vocabulary promises three ways
of reaching this verdict; the corpus holds two, and this is where that becomes visible.

Denominator 39 `insufficient_evidence` claims. The two cross-check causes are mutually exclusive.

| cause | claims | share | target |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `payment_precedes_subject` | 21 | 53.8% | 50% |
| `amount_mismatch` | 18 | 46.2% | 50% |
| `subject_not_evidenced` | 0 | — | **not drawn** |

`subject_not_evidenced` has no declared share, because nothing can plan a deliberately incomplete
claim; a consumer must not expect this cause in the corpus at any size. Both causes that *are* drawn
occur, which is what `config/policy.yaml` requires of the result rather than of the share.

For completeness, over all 359 claims: `limit_exhausted` 107, `mixed_items` 61,
`payment_precedes_subject` 21, `amount_mismatch` 18. The first two sum past their verdict's count
because one claim may carry both — 13 claims carry both, a combination for which the policy declares
no share.

## Capture channels, and the share of `content_complete: true` per channel

**This is the denominator of any character error rate on this corpus, and it is part of the result
rather than a caveat to it.** A CER is defined only where the text it is measured against is actually
in the picture. "CER on photo = X" is not a result; "CER on the 187 photographs of 205 whose text
survived" is.

Denominator 666 documents.

| channel | documents | share | `content_complete: true` | share complete | unmeasured |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `screenshot` | 231 | 34.7% | 231 of 231 | 100% | 0 |
| `scan` | 230 | 34.5% | 230 of 230 | 100% | 0 |
| `photo` | 205 | 30.8% | 187 of 205 | 91.2% | 0 |

18 photographs lost text off the frame — 15 across the right edge, 3 across the bottom. The other two
channels lost none: `screenshot` applies no geometry, and a flatbed's margin absorbs its own skew.

Two warnings that travel with these numbers:

- **Complete means the TEXT survived.** A capture that cut off a QR code or a stamp while keeping
  every character counts as complete, and truthfully — the scope is `content_bbox`, which covers text
  and nothing else.
- **The three channels are roughly equal by construction, not by observation.** They are drawn with
  equal weight, which is a placeholder rather than a measurement of how people submit documents.
  Every figure aggregated over documents is weighted by this marginal.

## Train / validation split

By **persona**, not by claim: annual limits are cumulative per persona, so a claim labelled
`partially_covered` for `limit_exhausted` carries that label because of that persona's earlier claims.
Partition by claim and a validation label becomes a function of training data. 85% of personas were
requested for train; the realized claim share differs because personas do not carry equal claim counts.

| side | personas | claims | documents | share of claims |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| train | 41 | 304 | 566 | 84.7% |
| validation | 7 | 55 | 100 | 15.3% |

Every claim and every document carries a side; nothing is unassigned.

**The partition is not stratified, and the validation side does not clear the per-class minimum.**
Every verdict and every document class the corpus contains occurs on both sides on this run — an
outcome, not a guarantee — but the sizes are what decide whether a figure may be quoted:

| class | train | validation |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| `invoice` | 262 | 45 |
| `bank_statement` | 133 | **21** |
| `payment_confirmation` | 129 | 24 |
| `fiscal_receipt` | 42 | **10** |

Denominators: 566 train documents and 100 validation documents. `fiscal_receipt` at 10 and
`bank_statement` at 21 are below 30. **A per-class figure computed on the validation side of this run
may not be quoted for either**, however healthy the corpus-level row looks.

The same applies to the CER denominator per side: on validation that is 27 complete photographs of 30,
which is already below the minimum before any class is considered.

| side | screenshot | scan | photo |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| train | 198 of 198 | 193 of 193 | 160 of 175 |
| validation | 33 of 33 | 37 of 37 | 27 of 30 |

## Other properties of this corpus

- **Claim shapes.** 307 claims carry two documents, 52 carry one. `linked` is true on the 307.
- **Cross-document checks.** 0 of the 307 multi-document claims contain a fiscal receipt — by
  construction rather than by outcome. A consumer measuring cross-document consistency has no case
  here in which a receipt is one of the two documents.
- **One currency and one language.** UAH and `uk` on all 666 documents. The corpus does not exercise
  either dimension: a per-value figure computed on it is the corpus average under another name.
- **Unidentified payers.** 13 of 666 documents carry a payer of `-`; the rate follows the declared
  initiation shares, not the run's size.
- **VAT summary row on the 52 fiscal receipts**, of which 47 carry a tax row. Paper — `photo` and
  `scan` — produced the `equals` form 28 times (19 + 9) and the spaced form never, which is the
  one-sided half of the asymmetric rule appearing in data. Electronic drew both: 14 spaced, 5 equals.
  5 receipts carry no tax row because the seller is not VAT-registered. The paper/electronic split
  here is the capture mix, so it measures that placeholder and not the world.

## What this corpus is not

Reading order matters here: the report above says what the corpus contains, and this section says what
a consumer must not conclude from it.

- It is not balanced across document classes, and nothing tried to make it so.
- It does not contain three of the six verdicts in the declared mix.
- It does not contain the `subject_not_evidenced` cause at any size.
- It does not exercise currency, language, or any jurisdiction other than UA.
- Its validation side is too small to carry a per-class figure for `fiscal_receipt` or
  `bank_statement`.
- Every artifact in it is synthetic and is not valid proof of payment.
