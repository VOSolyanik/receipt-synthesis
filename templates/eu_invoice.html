{# Cross-border invoice, English, EUR — the subject half of a euro claim. 🔴 WHY IT IS A SEPARATE TEMPLATE FROM THE PLATFORM RECEIPT, whose stylesheet it shares. The two documents are one house style and two document CLASSES, and the difference between them is exactly the fact this corpus is built on: a receipt states that money moved, an invoice asks for it. Sharing a body with conditionals would put the words «Amount paid» one boolean away from an invoice, and 📄 an invoice that recorded its own payment would contradict `document_evidence` in config/policy.yaml — the class proves the subject and NO payment. The stylesheet is shared because a platform's invoice and its receipt do look alike; the markup is not, because they say opposite things. 📄 WHAT THE LAW REQUIRES AND WHERE IT STOPS is argued once, in the `invoice` block of the EU jurisdiction in config/fiscal-rules.yaml, and not repeated here: Article 226 of Directive 2006/112/EC lists the particulars of an invoice under the VAT Directive. WHAT THE TOTALS BLOCK SAYS ABOUT THE TAX IS DRAWN — `content_builder.TaxTreatment`, derived from the buyer's country: the mass form adds the destination tax ON TOP (`total = subtotal + tax`), the out-of-scope form prints no row and the foot names Articles 44 and 59, and the reverse-charge form prints a zero row with its sentence. The rate parameters and their ⛔ not-legal-fact boundary are `tax_on_top` in config/fiscal-rules.yaml. ⛔ NO SUPPLIER ADDRESS AND NO VAT NUMBER ON EITHER SIDE — decisions, each recorded where its reason lives. ⛔ AND NO PAYMENT STATUS AT ANY RATE OF DRAWING: the page has a date by which it should be paid, which is a TERM OF THE OFFER, and nothing that says it was. Every element a consumer would extract carries data-field="". A field that is not on this document is omitted entirely rather than rendered empty, so no bounding box points at nothing. #}
