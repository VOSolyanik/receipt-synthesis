{# ONE FILE, TWO DOCUMENTS — an invoice and the bank confirmation that settled it, stitched into one multi-page file the way a claimant submits them. ⚠️ A MOCK-UP, on the same terms as the others here: not registered in `ARCHETYPES`, no builder, no run, and NO `data-field` attributes — the renderer collects no bounding box from it and nothing here becomes a label. See templates/README.md. 🔴 THE OTHER HALF OF THE SEGMENTATION CONTROL. `ua_insurance_contract` breaks «one page = one document» in one direction: one document across three pages. This breaks it in the other: two documents in one file. A file-splitting step measured on a corpus containing only the first would still be scoring against a guarantee — every cut it needs to make would be a cut it never has to refuse. Both directions or neither. 👁 AND IT IS AN OBSERVED PATTERN RATHER THAN A HYPOTHESIS: claimants really do staple the documents of one claim into a single PDF before submitting them. 🔴 THE STITCHING ADDS NOTHING, AND THAT IS THE WHOLE DIFFICULTY. Compare the contract: it numbers its own pages, repeats its own number on each of them, and carries a running head — the document tells you which pages belong together. Here there is no cover page, no continuous pagination, no unifying header, and no page numbering across the file. Each page carries only its own document's marks, and **nothing on the file says the two are different documents**. A segmenter has nothing to key on but the documents themselves. 🔴 NEITHER BODY IS REWRITTEN OR COPIED. The two sheets hold `ua_invoice` and `ua_bank_payment_confirmation` — the shipped templates, rendered from the shipped builders' contexts by the shipped renderer, written to disk and embedded as frames. The same route `ua_bank_receipt_in_app` takes, for the same reason and at the same cost: connecting this means the renderer learns to render documents inside another one. What it buys is that the pages of this file ARE the documents the dataset already contains, so a segmentation result on it is a result about those documents rather than about two files written to resemble them. THE TWO PAGES ARE ONE CLAIM, and the render script links them rather than hoping they agree: one vendor instance, one buyer, the invoice's own total handed to the confirmation as the transfer, the confirmation dated after the invoice, and the invoice's real number and date written into the payment purpose. That last one is the LINK — the thing a cross-document check keys on — and without the override the builder would have drawn an invoice number naming no document in the file. #} {# ONE `data-document` FOR THE WHOLE FILE.
